# Governance

Steading is early-stage, and deliberately a little anarchic.[^governance]

## Decisions

- **A light BDFL.** The project's founder acts as benevolent dictator to some degree. Steading holds strong opinions about software architecture, and doesn't compromise the integrity of the stack for the sake of consensus.[^governance]
- **Soft power.** Steading would rather show why an architectural decision is right than enforce it.[^governance]
- **No owners.** Work has no DRIs and no assigned owners.[^foundations]

## No permission needed

Modularity and interoperability are as much governance as architecture. You don't need anyone's permission to build your idea, or to replace a piece of the stack with something you think is better, and you don't need to share anyone's opinions to benefit from the work. Earn adoption, not allegiance.[^governance] See the [principles](principles.md#composable).

## Merging

Any maintainer in the [steading-ai](https://github.com/steading-ai) GitHub organization can merge; review is lightweight.[^governance] Each repository decides what merges without human review: see [the repository gates what merges](principles.md#agents-and-people).
